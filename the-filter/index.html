The nine rules are on this page, free, and they are the whole method. What is not on this page is the working: a before and after for each rule, taken from real drafts, so you can see what the fix actually costs a sentence. That is Don't Sound Like Me, $19.

This is not a detector, and it will not tell you whether a machine wrote your text. Nobody can tell you that honestly, and the tools that put a percentage on it are selling you a number they cannot stand behind. This counts nine specific habits, by name, and highlights each one so you can disagree with it. A high count on writing you are proud of means the count is wrong, not you.

The nine it is counting

  1. Lead with the reaction, not the setup.
  2. No em dashes. No exclamation marks. No dramatic fragments ("The result? Magic.").
  3. One specific thing, never a tidy list of three.
  4. Keep a note of doubt; don't fake certainty.
  5. A real question beats a clever conclusion.
  6. Never announce a feeling ("excited to", "thrilled to"). Say the thing.
  7. Keep the small connective words. Let it breathe.
  8. Cut the cleverest line, especially the "it's not X, it's Y" inversion. It's there to impress, not connect.
  9. If a sentence could come from any AI, delete it and try again.
